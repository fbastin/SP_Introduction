\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Motivation: financial planning}

(Birge and Louveaux, Section~1.2)

\begin{itemize}
\item
We invest an initial wealth $b$, and would like to reach a goal $G$ after $Y$ years.
\item
The portfolio is rebalanced every $v$ years: there are $H = Y/v$ investment periods, $\calT = \lbrace 1, \ldots, H \rbrace$, and an investment decision at the beginning of each period.
\item
$N$: set of available assets (stocks, bonds, \ldots).
\item
If the final wealth exceeds the goal $G$, the surplus earns interest at rate $q$.
\item
If it falls short of $G$, the shortfall must be borrowed at rate $r$ (typically, $r > q$).
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Variables and data}

\begin{itemize}
\item
$x_{it}$, $i \in N$, $t \in \calT$: amount invested in asset $i$ at the beginning of period $t$;
\item
$\omega_{it}$: return of asset $i$ during period $t$, i.e., the value at the end of the period of one dollar invested at its beginning;
\item
$y$: surplus over the goal at the end of the horizon;
\item
$w$: shortfall at the end of the horizon.
\end{itemize}

\mbox{}

When the returns are random, $x_{i2}, \ldots, x_{iH}$ are decided after observing the returns of the previous periods: they rebalance the portfolio, and are recourse actions toward the goal $G$.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Deterministic formulation}

\begin{align*}
\max\ & qy - rw \\
\mbox{s.t. } & \sum_{i \in N} x_{i1} = b, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{i,t-1}x_{i,t-1} = \sum_{i \in N} x_{it}, \quad t = 2, \ldots, H, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{iH}x_{iH} - y + w = G, \\
& x_{it} \geq 0, \quad i \in N,\ t \in \calT, \qquad y, w \geq 0.
\end{align*}

The wealth at the end of period $t-1$ is entirely reinvested at the beginning of period $t$.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Random returns}

\begin{itemize}
\item
The returns are not known in advance: they are random variables.
\item
Assume that, in each period $t$, the vector of returns $(\omega_{it})_{i \in N}$ takes one of $R$ possible values.
\item
A scenario is a sequence of realizations, one per period: there are $R^H$ scenarios.
\end{itemize}

Example: $R = 4$ and $H = 2$, hence 16 scenarios (one per row; each entry is the index of the realization in that period):

1 & 3 \\
1 & 4 \\
2 & 1 \\
\vdots & \vdots \\
4 & 4 \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{small}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Stochastic program formulation}

\begin{itemize}
\item
$S$: set of scenarios; $p_s$: probability of scenario $s \in S$;
\item
$\omega_{its}$: return of asset $i$ during period $t$ in scenario $s$;
\item
$x_{its}$: amount invested in asset $i$ at the beginning of period $t$ in scenario $s$;
\item
$y_s$, $w_s$: surplus and shortfall at the end of the horizon in scenario $s$.
\end{itemize}

\mbox{}

Each variable is copied once per scenario, and we maximize the expected value of $qy - rw$.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Stochastic version}

\begin{align*}
\max\ & \sum_{s \in S} p_s(qy_{\red s} - rw_{\red s}) \\
\mbox{s.t. } & \sum_{i \in N} x_{i1{\red s}} = b, \quad {\red \forall s \in S}, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{i,t-1,{\red s}}\, x_{i,t-1,{\red s}} = \sum_{i \in N} x_{it{\red s}}, \quad t = 2, \ldots, H,\ {\red \forall s \in S}, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{iH{\red s}}\, x_{iH{\red s}} - y_{\red s} + w_{\red s} = G, \quad {\red \forall s \in S}, \\
& x_{it{\red s}} \geq 0, \quad i \in N,\ t \in \calT,\ {\red s \in S}, \qquad y_{\red s}, w_{\red s} \geq 0, \quad {\red s \in S}.
\end{align*}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Scenario tree}

Conceptually, the sequence of events can be arranged in a tree: the root is the first decision, each branching is the realization of the returns of a period, and each leaf is a scenario.
Example ($R = 4$, $H = 2$):

\end{center}

The scenarios can have different probabilities, and the returns of successive periods can be dependent: the branching probabilities can differ from one node to another.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nonanticipativity}

\begin{itemize}
\item
Converting the deterministic model into the stochastic version is easy\ldots
\item
but the resulting model is {\red wrong}: $x_{its}$ may depend on the whole scenario $s$, including the returns of periods $t, \ldots, H$, unknown when $x_{its}$ is decided. The model sees the future.
\item
We have to enforce {\red nonanticipativity}: a decision may only depend on the information available when it is made.
\end{itemize}

Let $S_s^t$ be the set of scenarios identical to $s$ up to period $t-1$, i.e., indistinguishable from $s$ when the decisions of period $t$ are made ($S_s^1 = S$).
We impose
\[
x_{its} = x_{its'}, \quad \forall i \in N,\ t \in \calT,\ s \in S,\ s' \in S_s^t.
\]
The final $y_s$ and $w_s$ are decided once everything is known: they need no such constraint.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nonanticipativity (cont'd)}

Equivalently, as every $p_s > 0$,
\[
\Bigl( \sum_{s' \in S_s^t} p_{s'} \Bigr) x_{its} = \sum_{s' \in S_s^t} p_{s'} x_{its'}, \quad \forall i, t, s,
\]
that is,
\[
x_{its} = \frac{\sum_{s' \in S_s^t} p_{s'} x_{its'}}{\sum_{s' \in S_s^t} p_{s'}} = \EE\left[ x_{its'} \mid s' \in S_s^t \right], \quad \forall i, t, s.
\]
Each decision equals its conditional expectation given the information available when it is made: if all the decisions of a group of indistinguishable scenarios equal the group average, they are all equal.
This form underlies progressive hedging (Lagrangian methods slides).

\mbox{}

There are many other formulations, for instance equalities between consecutive scenarios of each group (next slide).

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nodal approach}

\begin{itemize}
\item
Alternatively, define one vector of decisions per node of the tree: the decision made given the realizations observed up to this node.
\item
Nonanticipativity is then built in: all the scenarios going through a node share its decision.
\item
$\calL$: set of decision nodes, with root $1$; the leaves are the scenarios $s \in S$.
\item
$a(k)$: parent of node $k$ (or of leaf $s$), i.e., its ancestor at the previous stage; $\omega_{ik}$: return of asset $i$ on the branch from $a(k)$ to $k$.
\item
Fewer variables, $|N|\,|\calL|$ instead of $|N|\,H\,|S|$, and no nonanticipativity constraint.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nodal formulation}

\begin{align*}
\max\ & \sum_{s \in S} p_s (qy_s - rw_s) \\
\mbox{s.t. } & \sum_{i \in N} x_{i1} = b, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{ik}\, x_{i,a(k)} = \sum_{i \in N} x_{ik}, \quad \forall k \in \calL \setminus \lbrace 1 \rbrace, \\
& \sum_{i \in N} \omega_{is}\, x_{i,a(s)} - y_s + w_s = G, \quad \forall s \in S, \\
& x_{ik} \geq 0, \quad i \in N,\ k \in \calL, \qquad y_s, w_s \geq 0, \quad s \in S.
\end{align*}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Multi-period production planning}

\begin{itemize}
\item
A firm produces several goods over several periods.
\item
Production uses resources (for instance, labor and machine hours); the amount of each resource needed per unit of each product is known.
\item
The demand of each period is random, and must be met: missing products are bought from an external source, and unsold ones are stored. Both have a cost.
\item
Additional resources (overtime, machine hours) can be bought, within limits.
\item
Changes in the workforce from one period to the next have a cost (hiring, firing).
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Decision problem}

\begin{itemize}
\item
We decide now, for every period:
\begin{itemize}
\item
the quantity of each product to manufacture;
\item
the additional resources to use;
\item
the hiring and firing of staff.
\end{itemize}
\item
At each period, the random demand is observed.
\item
Then, we decide how much to store, and how much to buy from the external source.
\end{itemize}

\mbox{}

The production plan is decided once, but the storage and purchase decisions of period $t$ may only use the demands of periods $1, \ldots, t$: the problem is {\red multistage}.

\medskip

This matters when buying ahead can pay, for instance if purchase prices rise faster than storage costs: how much to buy and store then depends on the forecast of the future demands.
If buying ahead never pays, buying only the deficit is optimal, whatever the future demands.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Sets and variables}

\begin{enumerate}
\item
{\red Sets}:
\begin{itemize}
\item
$\calT = \lbrace 1, \ldots, H \rbrace$: periods;
\item
$N$: products;
\item
$M$: resources; $L \in M$ is the labor.
\end{itemize}
\item
{\red Variables}:
\begin{itemize}
\item
$x_{jt}$: production of product $j \in N$ in period $t \in \calT$;
\item
$u_{it}$: additional amount of resource $i \in M$ bought for period $t$;
\item
$z_t^+$, $z_t^-$: increase and decrease of the workforce from period $t-1$ to period $t$, the workforce being the labor required by the production;
\item
$y_{jt}^+$: surplus of product $j$ at the end of period $t$, stored; $y_{jt}^-$: deficit, bought from the external source.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Parameters}

\begin{itemize}
\item
$\alpha_{jt}$, $\beta_{it}$, $\gamma_t^+$, $\gamma_t^-$, $\delta_{jt}^+$, $\delta_{jt}^-$: unit costs of the variables above;
\item
$\omega_{jt}$: random demand of product $j \in N$ in period $t \in \calT$;
\item
$a_{ij}$: amount of resource $i \in M$ required to produce one unit of product $j \in N$;
\item
$b_{it}$: amount of resource $i \in M$ available in period $t \in \calT$;
\item
$U_{it}$: upper bound on $u_{it}$;
\item
$y_{j0}^+$: initial inventory of product $j$.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Model}

{\small
\begin{multline*}
\min \sum_{j \in N} \sum_{t \in \calT} \alpha_{jt} x_{jt} + \sum_{i \in M}
\sum_{t \in \calT} \beta_{it} u_{it} + \sum_{t=2}^{H} \left(
  \gamma^+_t z^+_t + \gamma^-_t z^-_t \right) \\
+ \EE_{\bsomega} \left[ \min \sum_{j \in N} \sum_{t \in \calT}
    \left( \delta_{jt}^+y_{jt}^+ + \delta_{jt}^-y_{jt}^- \right) \right]
\end{multline*}
\begin{align*}
\mbox{s.t. } \sum_{j \in N} a_{ij} x_{jt} & \leq b_{it}+u_{it}, \quad 0 \leq u_{it} \leq U_{it}, \quad \forall i \in M,\ t \in \calT,\\
z^+_t - z^-_t &= \sum_{j \in N} a_{Lj}(x_{jt} - x_{j,t-1}), \quad t = 2, \ldots, H, \qquad x, z^{\pm} \geq 0, \\
x_{jt} + y_{j,t-1}^+ - y_{jt}^+ + y_{jt}^- &= \omega_{jt}, \quad \forall j \in N,\ t \in \calT, \qquad y^{\pm} \geq 0.
\end{align*}
Last constraints: demand satisfaction (inventory balance).
Inner $\min$: $y_{jt}^{\pm}$ depends only on the demands up to period $t$.
}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Extensive form of the recourse model}

{\small
\begin{multline*}
\min \sum_{j \in N} \sum_{t \in \calT} \alpha_{jt} x_{jt} + \sum_{i \in M}
\sum_{t \in \calT} \beta_{it} u_{it} + \sum_{t=2}^{H} \left(
  \gamma^+_t z^+_t + \gamma^-_t z^-_t \right) \\
+ {\red \sum_{s \in S} p_s} \sum_{j \in N} \sum_{t \in \calT}
    \left( \delta_{jt}^+y_{jt{\red s}}^+ + \delta_{jt}^-y_{jt{\red s}}^- \right)
\end{multline*}
\begin{align*}
\mbox{s.t. } \sum_{j \in N} a_{ij} x_{jt} & \leq b_{it}+u_{it}, \quad 0 \leq u_{it} \leq U_{it}, \quad \forall i \in M,\ t \in \calT,\\
z^+_t - z^-_t &= \sum_{j \in N} a_{Lj}(x_{jt} - x_{j,t-1}), \quad t = 2, \ldots, H, \qquad x, z^{\pm} \geq 0, \\
x_{jt} + y_{j,t-1,{\red s}}^+ - y_{jt{\red s}}^+ + y_{jt{\red s}}^- &= \omega_{jt{\red s}}, \quad \forall j \in N,\ t \in \calT,\ {\red s \in S}, \qquad y^{\pm} \geq 0, \\
{\red y_{jts}^{\pm}} &{\red = y_{jts'}^{\pm}} \quad \text{if } s \text{ and } s' \text{ have the same demands up to period } t.
\end{align*}
}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Multistage linear programs}

A {\red stage} is a decision time.
Decisions and observations alternate,
\[
x_1 \ \to\ \xi_2 \ \to\ x_2 \ \to\ \xi_3 \ \to\ \cdots \ \to\ \xi_H \ \to\ x_H,
\]
and $x_t$ may only depend on the history $\xi_{[t]} = (\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_t)$, $\xi_1$ being deterministic.
Over the horizon $t = 1, \ldots, H$, the multistage stochastic linear program is
\[
\min_{x_1 \geq 0}\ c_1^T x_1 + \EE\Bigl[ \min_{x_2 \geq 0} c_2^T x_2 + \EE\bigl[ \cdots + \EE[ \min_{x_H \geq 0} c_H^T x_H ] \bigr] \Bigr]
\]
subject to $W_1 x_1 = h_1$ and $W_t x_t = h_t(\xi_t) - T_{t-1}(\xi_t) x_{t-1}$, $t = 2, \ldots, H$ ($c_t$ may also depend on $\xi_t$).
\begin{itemize}
\item
Each expectation is conditional on the history, and each $\min$ over $x_t$ is taken once $\xi_t$ is observed, before $\xi_{t+1}$: the nesting enforces nonanticipativity.
\item
For $H = 2$, this is the two-stage recourse problem, with $A = W_1$, $b = h_1$, $y = x_2$, $q = c_2$, $W = W_2$, $h = h_2$, $T = T_1$.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Financial planning as a multistage program}

Stages $t = 1, \ldots, H$: the investments $x_t = (x_{it})_{i \in N}$; stage $H + 1$: $x_{H+1} = (y, w)$.
Between stages $t-1$ and $t$, the returns of period $t-1$ are observed: $\xi_t = (\omega_{i,t-1})_{i \in N}$.
With $\mathbf{1}$ the vector of ones (maximizing $qy - rw$ is minimizing $-qy + rw$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
stage & $c_t$ & $W_t$ & $h_t$ & $T_{t-1}(\xi_t)$ \\
\hline
$t = 1$ & $0$ & $\mathbf{1}^T$ & $b$ & -- \\
$t = 2, \ldots, H$ & $0$ & $\mathbf{1}^T$ & $0$ & $-\xi_t^T$ \\
$t = H + 1$ & $(-q, r)$ & $(-1, 1)$ & $G$ & $\xi_{H+1}^T$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For instance, $W_t x_t = h_t - T_{t-1} x_{t-1}$ reads $\mathbf{1}^T x_t = \xi_t^T x_{t-1}$ for $2 \leq t \leq H$: the wealth is reinvested.
\begin{itemize}
\item
$H$ periods, but $H + 1$ stages: the surplus and the shortfall are a last recourse decision, made once all the returns are known.
\item
The randomness lies in the technology matrices only.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Potential solution techniques}

Adapted from Jalal Kazempour, \url{https://www.jalalkazempour.com/teaching}

\begin{itemize}
\item
{\red Direct solution}: solve the extensive form with a linear programming solver; its size grows exponentially with the number of stages.
\item
{\red Linear decision rules}: restrict each decision to an affine function of the uncertain parameters observed so far, which gives an upper bound, for a minimization.
If the support of $\bsxi$ is a polytope, and the constraints must hold for every $\xi$ in it, duality yields a linear program whose size grows only polynomially with the number of stages (Kuhn, Wiesemann and Georghiou, 2011).
\item
{\red Nested decomposition} (nested Benders): fixes the decisions of the earlier stages (complicating variables), and decomposes the problem by node of the tree.
\item
{\red Lagrangian relaxation}: relaxes the nonanticipativity constraints (complicating constraints), and decomposes the problem by scenario.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Dynamic programming recursion}

Cost-to-go functions, with $\calQ_{H+1} \equiv 0$:
\begin{align*}
V_t(x_{t-1}, \xi_{[t]}) &= \min_{x_t \geq 0} \left\{ c_t^Tx_t + \calQ_{t+1}(x_t, \xi_{[t]}) \ :\ W_t x_t = h_t(\xi_t) - T_{t-1}(\xi_t)x_{t-1} \right\}, \\
\calQ_{t+1}(x_t, \xi_{[t]}) &= \EE\left[ V_{t+1}(x_t, \xi_{[t+1]}) \mid \xi_{[t]} \right].
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item
$V_t$: optimal cost of stages $t, \ldots, H$, given the previous decision and the history; $\calQ_{t+1}$: the {\red expected cost-to-go}, its expectation given the history.
\item
The problem is $\min \lbrace c_1^T x_1 + \calQ_2(x_1, \xi_1) : W_1 x_1 = h_1,\ x_1 \geq 0 \rbrace$: a two-stage problem whose recourse function is itself defined by a multistage problem.
\item
On a finite tree, there is one function $\calQ_{t+1}(\cdot, \xi_{[t]})$ per node of stage $t$, convex and piecewise linear on its domain.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nested decomposition}

The nested L-shaped method (Birge, 1985) applies the L-shaped method recursively on the tree.
Node $k$ at stage $t$, {\red master} of its children and {\red subproblem} of its parent $a(k)$, solves the nested L-shaped decomposition subproblem $NLSD(t,k)$:
\begin{align*}
\min\nolimits_{x_{t,k} \geq 0,\, \theta_{t,k}}\ & c_{t,k}^T x_{t,k} + \theta_{t,k} \\
\mbox{s.t. }\ & W_t x_{t,k} = h_{t,k} - T_{t-1,k}\, \hat{x}_{t-1,a(k)}, \quad (\pi_{t,k}) \\
& D_{t,k,\ell}\, x_{t,k} \geq d_{t,k,\ell},\ \ell = 1, \ldots, r_{t,k}, \quad (\rho_{t,k,\ell}) \quad \text{feasibility cuts}, \\
& E_{t,k,\ell}\, x_{t,k} + \theta_{t,k} \geq e_{t,k,\ell},\ \ell = 1, \ldots, s_{t,k}, \quad (\sigma_{t,k,\ell}) \quad \text{optimality cuts}.
\end{align*}
\begin{small}
\begin{itemize}
\item
$c_{t,k}$, $h_{t,k}$, $T_{t-1,k}$: values of $c_t$, $h_t$, $T_{t-1}$ at node $k$; $\hat{x}_{t-1,a(k)}$: trial decision of the parent; $r_{t,k}$, $s_{t,k}$: numbers of cuts received so far;
\item
$\theta_{t,k}$ underestimates the expected cost-to-go at node $k$, $\calQ_{t+1}(x_{t,k}, \xi_{[t]})$; bounded below until its first optimality cut, like $\theta^0$ in the L-shaped method; $\theta_{H,k} \equiv 0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{small}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nested decomposition: cuts}

Let $v_{t,k}$ be the optimal value of node $k$, and $p(k, t \mid j, t-1)$ the probability of moving from node $j$ to its child $k \in \calD_t(j)$.
The optimality cut of node $j$ at stage $t-1$, a new row of $NLSD(t-1,j)$, averages the supporting hyperplanes of its children:
\[
\theta_{t-1,j} \geq \sum_{k \in \calD_t(j)} p(k, t \mid j, t-1) \left( v_{t,k} - \pi_{t,k}^T T_{t-1,k} \left( x_{t-1,j} - \hat{x}_{t-1,j} \right) \right).
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item
{\red Why the cut is valid}: with its current cuts, node $k$ defines a convex function of $x_{t-1}$, below $V_t(\cdot, \xi_{[t]})$ since $\theta_{t,k}$ underestimates.
By linear programming duality, $v_{t,k} - \pi_{t,k}^T T_{t-1,k}(x_{t-1} - \hat{x}_{t-1,j})$ supports this function at $\hat{x}_{t-1,j}$.
Averaging over the children gives an affine function below $\calQ_t(\cdot, \xi_{[t-1]})$.
\item
The slope only involves the multipliers $\pi_{t,k}$ of the constraints linking node $k$ to its parent; the cuts of node $k$ enter through $v_{t,k}$: information {\red propagates up the tree}.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nested decomposition: feasibility cuts}

If $NLSD(t,k)$ is infeasible for the trial decision $\hat{x}_{t-1,j}$ of its parent $j = a(k)$, the solver returns a {\red certificate of infeasibility} (Farkas' lemma, linear programming background notes): $\pi_{t,k}$ and $\rho_{t,k} \geq 0$ such that
\[
\pi_{t,k}^T W_t + \rho_{t,k}^T D_{t,k} \leq 0
\quad \text{and} \quad
\pi_{t,k}^T \left( h_{t,k} - T_{t-1,k}\, \hat{x}_{t-1,j} \right) + \rho_{t,k}^T d_{t,k} > 0,
\]
where $D_{t,k}$ and $d_{t,k}$ stack the feasibility cuts of node $k$ ($\theta_{t,k}$ plays no role).
\begin{small}
\begin{itemize}
\item
If $x_{t,k} \geq 0$ solves node $k$ for some $x_{t-1}$, then $\pi_{t,k}^T (h_{t,k} - T_{t-1,k} x_{t-1}) + \rho_{t,k}^T d_{t,k} \leq (\pi_{t,k}^T W_t + \rho_{t,k}^T D_{t,k})\, x_{t,k} \leq 0$.
Hence the {\red feasibility cut}, a new row of $NLSD(t-1,j)$,
\[
\pi_{t,k}^T T_{t-1,k}\, x_{t-1,j} \geq \pi_{t,k}^T h_{t,k} + \rho_{t,k}^T d_{t,k},
\]
satisfied by every decision that keeps node $k$ feasible, and violated by $\hat{x}_{t-1,j}$.
\item
If the parent becomes infeasible, it sends a feasibility cut to its own parent; if the first stage does, the problem is infeasible.
None is needed with relatively complete recourse, as in the financial example.
\end{itemize}
\end{small}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Nested decomposition: forward and backward passes}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.62\textwidth}
\small
{\red Forward pass}: solve the first-stage problem, send $\hat{x}_1$ to its children, solve them, and so on down to stage $H$.
An infeasible node sends a feasibility cut to its parent, and a new forward pass starts.
Otherwise, the trial decisions form a nonanticipative policy, whose expected cost is an {\red upper bound}.

\medskip

{\red Backward pass}: for $t = H, \ldots, 2$, the nodes of stage $t$ send optimality cuts to their parents, solved again with these cuts.
The first-stage value is a {\red lower bound}.

\medskip

Stop when the bounds meet.
On a finite tree, with bounded $x_t$, only finitely many distinct cuts exist: finite convergence (Birge and Louveaux, Section~6.1, Theorem~1).
Other sequencing protocols change direction before reaching the last or the first stage.

\smallskip

Julia code: \texttt{code/nested\_benders.ipynb}.
\end{column}

\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Beyond nested decomposition}

The scenario tree grows exponentially with the number of stages, and so does the number of problems solved at each iteration of nested decomposition.

\begin{itemize}
\item
{\red Stochastic dual dynamic programming} (Pereira and Pinto, 1991; SDDP slides): under stagewise independence, all the nodes of a stage share the same expected cost-to-go function, hence the same cuts, and the forward pass samples scenarios instead of enumerating the tree.
Extensions handle Markovian processes, on a lattice.
\item
{\red Lagrangian methods} (progressive hedging; Lagrangian methods slides): relax the nonanticipativity constraints, and decompose the problem by scenario, each subproblem being a deterministic multistage problem.
They do not require a Markovian process, but there is one subproblem per scenario, and many iterations can be needed to restore nonanticipativity.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}
